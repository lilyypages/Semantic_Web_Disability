Sensory Disability

Hearing Impairment

Explore an organized overview of hearing-related characteristics, assessment, and support. Information should be interpreted in the context of each child's individual needs.

← Back to library
01 · Understand

Characteristics / Signs

Information about hearing-related signs and characteristics, such as differences in responding to sounds or following spoken communication. These observations alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

  • Difficulty responding to sounds May not turn toward voices or loud noises
  • Delayed speech development Language milestones appear slower than expected
  • Frequent requests for repetition Struggles to follow spoken instructions in noise
02 · Context

Causes / Risk Factors

Possible congenital, genetic, infectious, or acquired factors, with careful distinction between established causes and associated risk factors.

  • Genetic factor Established cause · inherited or de novo
  • Congenital infection Established cause · e.g. rubella, CMV
  • Chronic middle-ear infection Associated risk factor · acquired
  • Ototoxic medication exposure Associated risk factor · acquired
03 · Assessment

Assessment / Diagnosis

Overview of relevant hearing assessments, what an assessment may involve, and which qualified professionals are responsible for evaluation.

  • Newborn hearing screening OAE or AABR · early identification
  • Pure-tone audiometry Behavioral response · age-appropriate
  • Auditory brainstem response (ABR) Objective measure · infants and non-cooperative children
  • Tympanometry Middle-ear function screening
04 · People

Relevant Professionals

Professionals who may be involved in assessment and ongoing support, depending on the child's needs and local care pathway.

  • Audiologist Hearing assessment and device fitting
  • ENT specialist Ear, nose, and throat evaluation
  • Speech-language therapist Communication and language support
  • Educational audiologist Classroom listening support
05 · Everyday life

Management / Support

Communication access, family support, environmental adjustments, and coordination between home, school, and professionals.

  • Communication access at home Visual cues, reduced background noise
  • Environmental adjustments Front seating, clear sight lines
  • Care coordination Shared plan between family, school, and clinicians
06 · Intervention

Therapy / Intervention

Possible intervention approaches. Selection should be individualized and discussed with qualified professionals.

  • Speech and language therapy Targets expressive and receptive language
  • Auditory-verbal therapy Listening and spoken language development
  • Sign language support Visual communication option
07 · Development

Developmental Impact

How hearing-related needs may interact with communication, language, social participation, and learning — while recognizing that outcomes vary widely between children.

  • Language acquisition May be slower without early support
  • Social participation Peer interaction can require extra strategies
  • Learning access Classroom listening and instruction access
08 · Education

Education / Learning Support

Examples of classroom accommodations, accessible communication, teacher collaboration, and individualized learning support.

  • Classroom accommodations Preferred seating, visual aids, written instructions
  • Teacher collaboration Shared understanding of the child's needs
  • Individualized learning support Targeted to the child's communication profile
09 · Tools

Assistive Devices / Technology

Assistive listening devices and communication technologies where appropriate, selected with a qualified professional.

  • Hearing aid Amplification · fitted by audiologist
  • Cochlear implant For eligible children · specialist evaluation
  • FM / remote microphone system Classroom listening support
10 · Family perspective

Challenges / Support Needs

Common areas where children and families may need practical, educational, social, or emotional support.

  • Communication frustration Both child and family may experience it
  • Access to services Availability and continuity of support
  • Family emotional support Peer support and counselling where available
